# GENESIS.md

## Section 1 — Purpose

**DIGDUG.DO** is a permissionless protocol designed to distribute digital assets to real participants through provable on-chain activity rather than discretionary allocation, centralized decision-making, or opaque incentives.

The protocol exists to solve a simple but persistent problem in crypto:

> **Value distribution is either centralized, inefficient, or unprovable.**

Most token distributions rely on:

* private allocations,
* airdrops vulnerable to bots,
* marketing spend with unverifiable outcomes,
* or trusted intermediaries who decide who receives value and why.

DIGDUG.DO replaces these mechanisms with a neutral, rule-based system where:

* participants earn access to rewards by performing verifiable actions,
* sponsors distribute tokens without controlling recipients,
* and all economic activity is recorded immutably from the moment of launch.

At its core, DIGDUG.DO is not a game, a faucet, or a marketing tool.

It is a **distribution primitive**.

---

### 1.1. USDDD and Economic Intent

The protocol introduces **USDDD**, a USD-denominated unit used internally to coordinate participation, access, and accounting within DIGDUG.DO.

USDDD is designed as:

* a stable unit of account,
* a precursor to a fully realized stablecoin,
* and a mechanism to measure real usage before speculative markets are introduced.

From genesis, USDDD exists **on-chain**, with all minting, spending, and treasury accumulation publicly observable. There is no pre-history, no off-chain accounting phase, and no retroactive migration.

USDDD is not introduced to extract value from participants.
It exists to **measure behavior**, **regulate access**, and **prove demand** through use.

---

### 1.2. Neutrality as a Principle

DIGDUG.DO does not choose winners.

The protocol does not:

* curate which users receive rewards,
* favor specific sponsors,
* or intervene in distribution outcomes once rules are set.

Any sponsor willing to:

* provide real tokens,
* fund the gas costs of distribution,
* and accept the protocol’s constraints,

may participate.

Any user willing to:

* engage with the protocol honestly,
* follow the access rules,
* and accept the probabilistic nature of rewards,

may participate.

This neutrality is deliberate and foundational.

---

### 1.3. A Single Launch

DIGDUG.DO launches **once**.

There is no test phase visible to the public, no provisional ledger, and no transitional snapshot that asks participants to trust off-chain history.

From the first interaction:

* balances are real,
* events are immutable,
* and the system is live.

Everything that follows is growth, refinement, and scale — not validation.

---

### 1.4. What DIGDUG.DO Is Not

DIGDUG.DO is not:

* a promise of profit,
* a yield product,
* a speculative vehicle,
* or a substitute for market discovery.

It does not guarantee rewards, returns, or outcomes.

It guarantees only this:

> **Rules are enforced as written, and history cannot be rewritten.**

---

**End of Section 1**

## Section 2 — Genesis Principles

DIGDUG.DO is built on a small number of non-negotiable principles. These principles define what the protocol *can* become — and just as importantly, what it can never become.

They are enforced not by intention, but by design.

---

### 2.1. Immutability from Birth

DIGDUG.DO begins with a single, irreversible state.

There is no private test ledger, no off-chain rehearsal, and no hidden pre-launch accounting. The first block that recognizes DIGDUG.DO marks the beginning of its history — and that history is final.

All balances, distributions, and treasury movements exist on-chain from genesis onward.

Nothing is imported.
Nothing is retroactively minted.
Nothing relies on trust in operators.

If a record does not exist on-chain, it does not exist.

---

### 2.2. Distribution Without Discretion

The protocol does not decide *who* receives value.

DIGDUG.DO enforces rules, not preferences. Once a sponsor configures a distribution campaign and commits funds, the protocol executes distribution automatically according to predefined mechanics.

There are:

* no allowlists curated by DIGDUG.DO,
* no manual overrides,
* no privileged recipients,
* no exceptions after deployment.

Sponsors relinquish control at the moment of commitment.
Participants accept uncertainty at the moment of engagement.

This removes favoritism and replaces it with probability.

---

### 2.3. Cost Must Precede Reward

Every reward distributed through DIGDUG.DO is backed by real cost.

For sponsors, this includes:

* the tokens being distributed,
* the gas required to deploy and maintain distribution contracts,
* and the gas consumed by participant claims.

For participants, this includes:

* limited access via USDDD,
* time constraints,
* cooldowns,
* and opportunity cost.

There are no free rewards created ex nihilo by the protocol itself.

DIGDUG.DO does not manufacture value.
It reallocates value supplied willingly by sponsors.

---

### 2.4. Measurement Before Monetization

The protocol prioritizes **proof of use** before **economic extraction**.

USDDD exists first as:

* a measurement tool,
* a coordination unit,
* and an access control mechanism.

Only after sustained, observable usage does the system permit expanded monetary behavior.

This ordering is deliberate.

Speculation follows utility — never the reverse.

---

### 2.5. Transparency as Default

All meaningful state transitions are publicly observable:

* USDDD minting and consumption,
* treasury accumulation,
* reward distributions,
* and sponsor campaign activity.

There are no hidden balances, shadow ledgers, or discretionary accounting layers.

Participants may not understand every mechanism — but they can verify every outcome.

---

### 2.6. Resistance to Capture

DIGDUG.DO is designed to resist control by:

* founders,
* sponsors,
* whales,
* or coordinated actors.

No single party can:

* redirect rewards,
* block participation,
* confiscate balances,
* or alter rules post-deployment.

Even the creator of the protocol is bound by the same constraints as every other participant.

Power is limited by architecture, not promises.

---

### 2.7. Voluntary Participation Only

Participation is never required.

Sponsors are not compelled to distribute tokens.
Participants are not compelled to engage.
Markets are not compelled to adopt USDDD.

DIGDUG.DO offers infrastructure — not obligation.

Those who find value will use it.
Those who do not, will ignore it.

The protocol remains indifferent.

---

### 2.8. No Narrative Dependence

DIGDUG.DO does not rely on storytelling, belief, or persuasion to function.

It does not require:

* community faith,
* brand loyalty,
* or ideological alignment.

If the protocol delivers value, it survives.
If it does not, it fades.

This is not a social experiment.

It is a system.

---

## Section 3 — Core Mechanics

USDDD is a **protocol-native access unit**, not a general-purpose currency.

It exists solely to regulate, measure, and price participation in DIGDUG.DO.

---

### 3.1. Key Properties

At genesis, USDDD is:

* **non-transferable**
* **non-withdrawable**
* **non-tradable**
* **non-custodial by users**
* **consumable only by the protocol**

USDDD **cannot leave DIGDUG.DO**.

It can only be:

1. minted by protocol rules, and
2. consumed to initiate digs.

There is no external market for USDDD.
There is no peer-to-peer movement.
There is no speculative utility.

USDDD represents **access**, not value storage.

---

### 3.2. Minting

USDDD is minted programmatically according to fixed protocol rules, including:

* new participant onboarding grants,
* limited replenishment mechanisms,
* and protocol-defined caps.

Minting is **not discretionary** and **not sponsor-controlled**.

All mint events are publicly observable.

---

### 3.3. Consumption

When a participant initiates a dig:

* the required USDDD is **irreversibly consumed**,
* it cannot be recovered, refunded, or reused,
* and it is permanently credited to the protocol treasury.

Consumption is final.

USDDD has no lifecycle beyond mint → consume → record.

---

### 3.4.Purpose

USDDD exists to make participation **measurable**.

Each unit consumed represents:

* one unit of participant demand,
* one unit of sponsor exposure,
* one unit of system throughput.

USDDD is deliberately constrained so that:

* it cannot distort incentives,
* it cannot be farmed for profit,
* and it cannot leak value outside the protocol.

---

## 3.5. Protocol Treasury (Revised)

The DIGDUG.DO treasury is an **accounting construct**, not a pool of user funds.

It represents the cumulative consumption of USDDD by participants.

---

### 3.6. Treasury Formation

Every time USDDD is consumed:

* the unit is credited to the protocol treasury,
* the treasury balance increases monotonically,
* and the system’s total usage increases by one unit.

The treasury:

* does not receive sponsor tokens,
* does not custody participant rewards,
* and does not interact with withdrawals.

It tracks **protocol demand**, not liquidity.

---

### 3.7. Treasury Characteristics

The treasury is:

* **append-only** (cannot decrease),
* **globally observable**,
* **not sponsor-owned**,
* **not participant-owned**.

It cannot be drained.
It cannot be redistributed.
It cannot be accessed by users.

Its sole function is **economic measurement**.

---

### 3.8. Economic Meaning

The treasury balance is a direct, immutable signal of:

* total participation,
* total access demand,
* and total protocol usage over time.

It forms the empirical basis for:

* later monetary policy,
* stablecoin issuance constraints,
* and system valuation.

The treasury does **not** imply backing, reserves, or redemption guarantees.

It implies **proof of use**.

---

## 3.9. Clarifying What USDDD Is *Not*

To avoid misinterpretation, USDDD is explicitly **not**:

* a stablecoin (at genesis),
* a payment instrument,
* a claim on protocol assets,
* a security,
* or a reward token.

It is an internal unit of account used to **price attention**.

---

## 3.10. Why This Matters

By separating:

* **access (USDDD)**,
* **rewards (sponsor tokens)**,
* and **value settlement (external chains)**,

DIGDUG.DO avoids:

* circular token economics,
* reflexive ponzi dynamics,
* and discretionary monetary control.

This separation is intentional and foundational.

## Section 4 — Lifecycle Stages

DIGDUG.DO evolves through clearly defined stages. These stages do not represent changes in legitimacy or truth, but changes in **capacity, openness, and scope**.

There is no migration between stages.
There is no reset.
There is no second genesis.

The system is real from the first block.

---

## 4.1. Stage I — Genesis (Launch)

**Objective:** Establish immutable history and prove real behavior
**Status:** Live on mainnet
**USDDD:** On-chain, constrained, access-only

Genesis begins at the moment DIGDUG.DO becomes publicly accessible.

From the first interaction:

* USDDD minting is real,
* USDDD consumption is final,
* treasury accounting is immutable,
* sponsor campaigns are autonomous,
* and rewards are real tokens.

There is no test environment visible to the public.

At this stage:

* USDDD exists only as a protocol access unit.
* USDDD cannot be transferred, withdrawn, or traded.
* Participation is limited by strict minting and replenishment rules.
* Treasury growth reflects real usage, not speculation.

The purpose of Genesis is **measurement**, not scale.

The system asks one question only:

> *Do participants engage when value distribution is fair and verifiable?*

---

## 4.2. Stage II — Expansion (Birth)

**Objective:** Increase throughput and resilience
**Status:** Same chain, same contracts, expanded parameters
**USDDD:** Still constrained, more expressive

Expansion does not introduce new legitimacy.

It introduces **capacity**.

During this stage:

* onboarding limits may increase,
* replenishment mechanics may become more flexible,
* sponsor tooling may expand,
* and anti-abuse mechanisms harden.

Treasury accounting continues unchanged.

No balances are migrated.
No history is rewritten.
No contracts are replaced.

USDDD remains:

* non-transferable,
* non-withdrawable,
* and confined to protocol use.

What changes is **volume**, not meaning.

---

## 4.3. Stage III — Open Life

**Objective:** Enable broader economic participation
**Status:** Widely adopted infrastructure
**USDDD:** Evolves into a fully realized monetary instrument

Only after sustained, observable usage does DIGDUG.DO consider expanding USDDD’s role beyond access.

Any such expansion:

* occurs through additive mechanisms,
* preserves historical meaning,
* and does not retroactively alter prior usage.

Possible evolutions may include:

* partial transferability,
* external integrations,
* market discovery,
* or governance participation.

These changes are **policy decisions**, not rewrites.

Genesis history remains authoritative.

---

## 4.4. What Never Changes

Across all stages:

* On-chain history remains final.
* Treasury accounting remains append-only.
* Sponsors cannot influence distribution outcomes post-deployment.
* Participants cannot extract value without cost.
* Founders cannot override rules.

Stage transitions expand what is *possible* — not what is *true*.

---

## 4.5. Failure Is an Acceptable Outcome

DIGDUG.DO does not guarantee progression between stages.

If:

* users do not engage,
* sponsors do not participate,
* or incentives fail,

the protocol remains idle.

No artificial stimulus is introduced to force growth.

A system that cannot survive without intervention should not survive at all.

---

## 4.6. Lifecycle Summary

DIGDUG.DO does not “launch, test, and relaunch”.

It:

1. **Begins**
2. **Observes**
3. **Expands**
4. **Lives**

Or it stops.

---

**End of Section 4**

## Section 5 — Founder Constraints & Governance

DIGDUG.DO is not governed by personal authority, reputation, or discretion.

It is governed by **constraints**.

The role of the founder exists only to **initialize** the system — not to control it indefinitely.

---

## 5.1. The Founder’s Role

The founder of DIGDUG.DO is the author of the initial ruleset and the deployer of the genesis contracts.

This role includes:

* defining protocol parameters at launch,
* selecting the initial execution environment,
* and publishing this Genesis document.

It does **not** include:

* discretionary control over user balances,
* discretionary control over sponsor distributions,
* or discretionary authority to rewrite economic history.

The founder’s authority ends where the protocol’s rules begin.

---

## 5.2. Explicit Founder Constraints

From genesis onward, the founder **cannot**:

* mint USDDD outside protocol-defined rules,
* redirect or confiscate participant rewards,
* access or drain the protocol treasury,
* alter sponsor campaign outcomes,
* or invalidate on-chain history.

There are no privileged keys that bypass execution logic.

If a rule is not encoded, it does not exist.

---

## 5.3. No Founder Allocation

DIGDUG.DO does not reserve a special allocation of USDDD for the founder.

USDDD is not a founder reward, incentive, or compensation mechanism.

Any USDDD consumed by the founder is subject to the same rules as every other participant.

This prevents:

* early extraction,
* hidden advantage,
* and narrative capture.

Founder benefit arises only from **system success**, not issuance privilege.

---

## 5.4. Treasury Stewardship

The protocol treasury is not a founder wallet.

It is a public, append-only accounting record representing cumulative protocol usage.

The founder may act as a **steward** of treasury policy only to the extent that such policy is:

* transparent,
* rule-bound,
* and publicly declared.

The founder may not:

* treat treasury balance as personal capital,
* withdraw treasury units for private use,
* or repurpose treasury accounting retroactively.

Any future use of treasury data or value must be governed by published policy.

---

## 5.5. Governance Evolution

DIGDUG.DO launches without complex governance.

This is intentional.

Early governance mechanisms are:

* brittle,
* easily captured,
* and poorly informed.

Governance evolves only after:

* sustained real usage,
* observable economic behavior,
* and demonstrated resistance to abuse.

Any future governance framework must:

* preserve genesis history,
* respect founder constraints,
* and remain subordinate to protocol rules.

Governance is additive — never retroactive.

---

## 5.6. Founder Privilege Decay

Founder influence is expected to **decrease over time**, not increase.

This decay may occur through:

* parameter freezing,
* multi-party oversight,
* contract immutability,
* or explicit delegation.

The founder does not choose permanence.

The system earns it.

---

## 5.7. No Emergency Powers

DIGDUG.DO does not include “emergency stop” mechanisms that allow unilateral intervention by the founder.

Failure modes are acknowledged and accepted.

If the protocol fails, it fails openly.

Rescue by discretion is indistinguishable from control.

---

## 5.8. Accountability to History

The founder is accountable to the protocol’s history, not its popularity.

All actions taken by the founder:

* are visible on-chain,
* are attributable,
* and remain permanently recorded.

The founder cannot revise intent after the fact.

This document exists so that:

* future participants can verify alignment,
* future contributors can evaluate continuity,
* and future scrutiny can assess integrity.

---

## 5.9. Closing Statement

DIGDUG.DO does not ask participants to trust the founder.

It asks them to trust **constraints enforced by code**.

The founder accepts stricter limits than any other participant because legitimacy flows downward, not upward.

## Section 6 — Sponsor Guarantees & Constraints

Sponsors participate in DIGDUG.DO to distribute tokens transparently and verifiably. In doing so, they accept a defined set of guarantees and constraints that ensure neutrality, fairness, and integrity of distribution.

Sponsors are not customers of discretion.
They are participants in a rules-based system.

---

## 6.1. Sponsor Eligibility

Any entity may act as a sponsor provided that:

* the tokens supplied are legitimate, transferable, and non-malicious,
* the underlying chain is supported by the protocol,
* the sponsor funds all required gas and distribution costs,
* and the sponsor accepts the protocol’s non-intervention guarantees.

DIGDUG.DO does not require sponsor identity disclosure beyond what is necessary for technical operation.

Participation is permissionless, subject only to protocol constraints.

---

## 6.2. Sponsor Commitments

By deploying a treasure campaign, a sponsor commits to:

* distributing tokens without selecting recipients,
* accepting probabilistic outcomes,
* funding all gas required for participant claims,
* and allowing the protocol to execute distribution autonomously.

Once a campaign is live, the sponsor relinquishes control over allocation outcomes.

Distribution is executed by code, not by preference.

---

## 6.3. No Recipient Control

Sponsors cannot:

* approve or deny individual claims,
* prioritize specific participants,
* block specific addresses,
* or retroactively alter distribution parameters.

DIGDUG.DO does not provide tools to curate or influence recipients after deployment.

This prevents targeted distribution, favoritism, and coercion.

---

## 6.4. Immutability of Campaign Logic

A treasure campaign’s core parameters are immutable once deployed, including:

* reward token identity,
* distribution curve,
* allocation limits,
* and claim constraints.

Sponsors may:

* pause campaigns,
* top up reward balances,
* or extend duration.

They may not:

* change reward logic,
* alter probabilities,
* or reverse completed distributions.

Immutability ensures credibility for participants.

---

## 6.5. Token Custody and Separation

DIGDUG.DO does not custody sponsor tokens.

Sponsor tokens:

* remain within sponsor-funded campaign contracts,
* are transferred directly to participants upon withdrawal,
* and are never pooled with protocol or treasury assets.

At no point does DIGDUG.DO:

* escrow sponsor funds,
* rehypothecate rewards,
* or intermediate settlement.

This separation is intentional and permanent.

---

## 6.6. Gas Responsibility

Sponsors are solely responsible for:

* contract deployment gas,
* claim execution gas,
* and any operational gas required by their campaign.

DIGDUG.DO does not subsidize sponsor distribution costs.

This ensures:

* honest cost signaling,
* resistance to spam campaigns,
* and sustainability at scale.

---

## 6.7. Campaign Outcomes and Reporting

Upon completion or exhaustion of a campaign, sponsors may observe:

* number of participants reached,
* total tokens distributed,
* distribution velocity,
* and time-to-completion.

These metrics are derived from on-chain events and are verifiable.

DIGDUG.DO does not guarantee marketing outcomes, community growth, or price impact.

It guarantees only accurate execution and transparent reporting.

---

## 6.8. Sponsor Disclaimers

By participating, sponsors acknowledge that:

* distribution through DIGDUG.DO does not guarantee holder retention,
* participants may immediately transfer or sell received tokens,
* market effects are outside the protocol’s control,
* and distribution is not a substitute for product-market fit.

DIGDUG.DO provides infrastructure — not endorsement.

---

## 6.9. No Special Treatment

Sponsors receive no preferential access to:

* protocol parameters,
* user data beyond public metrics,
* treasury influence,
* or governance privileges by default.

All sponsors operate under identical constraints.

Any future premium features must:

* be additive,
* be transparent,
* and not affect distribution fairness.

---

## 6.10. Closing Statement

Sponsors using DIGDUG.DO trade control for credibility.

They give up discretion in exchange for:

* transparent execution,
* verifiable reach,
* and provable distribution.

This exchange is deliberate.

DIGDUG.DO is designed to distribute value fairly — not to optimize sponsor convenience.

Participation implies acceptance of both.

Any participant may engage with DIGDUG.DO provided they:

Access is granted by rules, not discretion.

All interactions are executed strictly according to protocol logic.

* no reward,

on-chain history remains intact and verifiable.

Participants rely on code execution, not operator availability.

## 7.9. Closing Statement

DIGDUG.DO guarantees fairness of execution — not fairness of outcome.

Participants are treated equally by rules, not rewarded equally by chance.

Those who engage do so with full visibility into constraints and consequences.
